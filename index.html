A short story of Texas Hold'em

The River

In Texas Hold'em the last card dealt is called the river. This page is about the game itself: how it's played, and how a card game from a small Texas town ended up in Las Vegas.

The game in thirty seconds

2

cards for you

Every player gets two cards face down. Nobody else sees them.

5

cards for everyone

The dealer puts five shared cards in the middle: three at once (the flop), then one (the turn), then the last one (the river).

1

best hand of five

Your hand is the best five cards out of the seven you can use. Before each new card there is a round of betting, and anyone can fold.

From Robstown to Las Vegas

1900s

A small town in Texas

The game most likely started in Robstown, a town near Corpus Christi, early in the twentieth century. In 2007 the Texas Legislature officially recognized Robstown as the birthplace of Texas Hold'em.

1967

Road gamblers come to Vegas

A group of Texas players, among them Doyle Brunson, Amarillo Slim and Crandell Addington, brought the game to Las Vegas. At first only one casino, the Golden Nugget, dealt it.

1970

The first World Series

Benny Binion invited the best players to his Horseshoe casino. The players voted for the winner, Johnny Moss. From 1971 the Main Event became a No-Limit Hold'em tournament, and it still is.

2003

An accountant wins

Chris Moneymaker, an accountant from Tennessee, won the Main Event and $2.5 million after getting in through an $86 online qualifier. The next year, the number of people playing online exploded. It's still called the Moneymaker effect.
